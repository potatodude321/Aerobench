# Benchmarks

Level L3 of the [validation ladder](validation.md): canonical cases with published
measurements, run through the normal application pipeline (geometry generated by the
application, automatic mesh plan, snappyHexMesh, simpleFoam, AeroBench force reduction and
convergence assessment). Nothing is tuned to hit a reference. Every run writes a JSON
record (`<project>/validation/<case>.json`) with metrics, checks, comparisons, convergence
status, cell count, wall time, OpenFOAM version and hardware, so results can be archived
and compared between releases and machines.

```bash
aerobench benchmark list
aerobench benchmark run ahmed25 --level medium --cores 8 --out D:\AeroBench\benchmarks
aerobench benchmark run ahmed35 --level medium --cores 8 --out D:\AeroBench\benchmarks
```

Levels: `coarse` (background cell 0.16 m, body surface level 4–5, 1 200 iterations),
`medium` (0.12 m, level 5–6, 2 000 iterations), `fine` (0.08 m, level 5–6, 3 000
iterations). All levels use three relative boundary layers.

## Status values

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `pass` | Run completed, convergence `converged` or `converged_oscillating`, every acceptance quantity inside its band |
| `outside_band` | Converged, but at least one quantity outside its band |
| `unconverged` | Completed but the convergence criteria were not met — **never a pass, whatever the value** |
| `not_run` | The run did not complete |

## Cases

### Ahmed body, 25° slant (`ahmed25`)

Ahmed, Ramm & Faltin (SAE 840300, 1984) geometry generated from the published dimensions
(L = 1.044 m, W = 0.389 m, H = 0.288 m, front radius 0.100 m, slant length 0.222 m,
ground clearance 0.050 m, four cylindrical stilts). 40 m/s (Re_L ≈ 2.8 × 10⁶), stationary
ground, steady k-ω SST. Reference area W × H = 0.1120 m² (stilts excluded), as in the
experiments.

| Quantity | Reference | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Cd | 0.285 | Ahmed, Ramm & Faltin (1984) |
| Cd | 0.299 | Meile et al. (2011), 40 m/s |
| Cl | 0.345 | Meile et al. (2011), 40 m/s (informative, no band) |
| Acceptance band | 0.25 ≤ Cd ≤ 0.34 | Both measurements plus the known steady-RANS over-prediction at this angle |

The 25° slant sits just below the critical angle, with a partially attached slant flow and
strong C-pillar vortices. Steady RANS is known to over-predict the slant separation and
therefore drag; a result inside the band is consistent with published RANS studies, not
proof of accuracy for every configuration. For orientation: a published k-ω SST study with
about 16.6 million cells reports Cd = 0.313 and Cl = 0.355 against Meile's 0.299 and 0.345
(SimFlow Ahmed-body validation).

### Ahmed body, 35° slant (`ahmed35`)

Same set-up with a 35° slant. Above the critical angle (~30°) the slant flow separates
fully and drag falls below the 25° value. Qualitative check: Cd(35°) < Cd(25°) at the same
level.

## Recorded results

Development records (JSON in [benchmark-records/](benchmark-records/)), OpenFOAM v2606
(Linux packages) on a 2-core x86-64 virtual machine
with 7.8 GB memory, AeroBench 0.1.0. They document the behaviour of the pipeline; they are
**not** release validation records (see "Release records" below). Coefficients are means
over the final 20 % of iterations.

| Case | Level | Cells | Iterations | Wall time | Cd | Cl | Convergence | Status | Cd vs 0.285 / 0.299 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ahmed25 | coarse | 237 020 | 1 200 | 9 min | 0.363 | 0.348 | converged | outside_band | +27.3 % / +21.3 % |
| ahmed25 | medium | 1 190 476 | 2 000 | 2 h 9 min | 0.335 | 0.356 | not converged | unconverged | +17.7 % / +12.2 % |
| ahmed35 | coarse | 238 507 | 1 200 | 9 min | 0.461 | 0.485 | not converged | unconverged | — |

Reading these results:

* **Mesh trend.** Refining from coarse to medium lowers Cd by 0.028 towards the
  measurements (the 16.6 M-cell study above reaches 0.313). Neither level is mesh-independent;
  the `fine` level and a three-level grid-convergence study (`mesh.base_cell_size` study,
  GCI per Celik et al. 2008) are needed before quoting a mesh-converged value.
* **Medium is not converged, and the record says so.** Over the last 400 iterations Cd
  swings within 0.329–0.342 (standard deviation 0.0039: above the 0.002 "settled" threshold,
  inside the 0.010 bounded-oscillation band) while Cl swings within 0.288–0.422 (standard
  deviation 0.043, twice the 0.020 bounded band); the momentum residual fell by only 2.0
  orders. Drift is small (Cd 0.0007, Cl 0.0001 across the window): this is the trendless
  large oscillation of an unsteady separated slant flow that a steady solver cannot settle,
  not an unfinished transient, and the assessment reports it as such ("large oscillation
  without a trend", since criteria refinement in 0.1.0). The window-mean Cd = 0.335 lies
  inside the band, but the status is `unconverged`, so it is not counted as a pass. Lift is
  within 3 % of Meile's 0.345; given the oscillation amplitude this is agreement within the
  uncertainty, not evidence of accuracy.
* **Regime change not reproduced at the coarse level.** The 35° body gives Cd = 0.461 and
  Cl = 0.485 — *higher* than at 25°, where the experiments show a clear drop. Pressure drag
  (0.436) and the positive lift show that the slant flow stays largely attached in this
  solution, i.e. the coarse mesh puts the case on the wrong side of the critical angle; the
  coefficients also oscillate strongly without a trend (Cd ±0.014, Cl ±0.027). The geometry
  was checked (slant angle 35.0°, base area 0.0625 m², closed and outward-oriented). The
  coarse level is therefore not suitable for slant-separation physics; it remains useful as a
  quick pipeline check. Whether `medium`/`fine` capture the regime change is to be
  established by the release records — until then the qualitative check counts as failed.

## Release records

Before a release, run `ahmed25` and `ahmed35` at `medium` and `fine` on the reference
Windows workstations with the native OpenFOAM v2606 build and archive the JSON records
with the release (roadmap item 1). A release note quotes those records, including any
`outside_band` or `unconverged` status, verbatim.
